Three companies, three different stories

Revenue, profit and cash flow can tell different stories about the same company. I pulled five figures per company-year from their SEC 10-K filings and compared them.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • Jupyter
  • Excel
FY2023FY2024FY2025 Fiscal years end on different dates for each company.

Microsoft

Revenue rose from $211.9bn to $281.7bn, net income from $72.4bn to $101.8bn, and operating cash flow from $87.6bn to $136.2bn. Growth, profit and cash all moved together.

Tesla

Revenue stayed near $95–98bn while net income fell from $15.0bn to $3.9bn. Operating cash flow held at $13–15bn, so profit and cash flow can diverge sharply.

Apple

Net income dipped in FY2024 and recovered to $112.0bn in FY2025. Operating cash flow stayed above $110bn throughout, and liabilities fell to $285.5bn in FY2025.

How I built the dataset

Each row is one company and fiscal year, with the SEC 10-K it came from recorded beside it so any number can be checked. In pandas I added year-over-year growth for all five metrics, net margin, operating cash flow margin and liabilities as a share of assets.

Fiscal years don't line up: Microsoft ends 30 June, Tesla 31 December and Apple in late September. The analysis labels each figure with its own period instead of treating them as the same calendar year.

for metric, new in [
    ('Total Revenue ($m)', 'Revenue Growth (%)'),
    ('Net Income ($m)',    'Net Income Growth (%)'),
    # ... assets, liabilities, cash flow
]:
    df[new] = df.groupby('Company')[metric].pct_change() * 100

df['Net Margin (%)'] = df['Net Income ($m)'] / df['Total Revenue ($m)'] * 100

A chatbot that says what it can't do

The demo at the top of this page is a JavaScript port of my Python command-line chatbot. Both use the same filing data and the same rules.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • unittest

What it does

It finds the company, year and metric in a question, looks the figure up, and answers in USD billions with the fiscal year-end date. For net income and revenue "change" questions it compares the latest two fiscal years and reports both the dollar and percentage difference. It can also rank all three companies by 2025 revenue.

Anything it can't match gets a plain fallback pointing to help, rather than a guess.

It is a rule-based prototype, not a generative model. It matches keywords, covers three companies and three years, doesn't fetch live filings, and isn't investment advice. I documented these limits in the README.

Great Britain Olympic middle-distance medals

Loads the TidyTuesday Olympics dataset straight from GitHub, filters to Great Britain in the men's and women's 800m and 1,500m, and recodes missing medals as "no medal". It counts results per event and medal, then draws one panel per event with gold, silver and bronze in their own colours.

olympics %>%
  filter(team == "Great Britain", event %in% c(...)) %>%
  mutate(medal = ifelse(is.na(medal), "no medal", tolower(medal))) %>%
  group_by(event, medal) %>%
  summarise(individual_medal_count = n(), .groups = "drop")

Harry Kane season summary

Reads a season-by-season CSV and builds new columns: goals plus assists, goals without penalties, and goals minus expected goals to show over- and under-performance. It then summarises seasons played, appearances, total goals and average expected goals, and filters to seasons with more than 30 goal contributions.
